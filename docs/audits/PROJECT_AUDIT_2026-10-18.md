# Project consistency audit

Reviewed October 18, 2026. The project has useful trajectory and decision-analysis components, but its current files do not constitute one reproducible, validated perception-rating pipeline. The most consequential problems concern the interpretation of learned uncertainty, incorrect probability calculations, unequal baseline evaluation, and saved outputs that do not match current code.

P1 means a result-validity or workflow-blocking issue to resolve before relying on a regenerated leaderboard. P2 means a material correctness, interpretation, or reproducibility issue. Modeling limitations are distinguished from executable bugs below.

## Highest priority findings

### 1. P1 Training data cannot be rebuilt

`scripts/build_training_data.py:185` contains `'features': FEATURES,¡`. Parsing reproduces `SyntaxError: invalid character '¡' (U+00A1)`. Every invocation fails before any processing. Remove the stray character and check that the builder produces the expected schema before regenerating data.

### 2. P1 The LSTM uncertainty has no validated human perception interpretation

The LSTM receives reconstructed position, velocity, and constant acceleration at every timestep (`scripts/build_training_data.py:35`, `src/physics.py:107`). Those quantities already determine the endpoint analytically from the first valid state. Training optimizes endpoint-coordinate likelihood (`src/model.py:135`); it does not inject a sensory observation model or learn from batter perception measurements. Swing labels are saved but do not supervise this loss.

The resulting sigma describes model residual uncertainty conditional on retrospective fitted physics. Calling it human visual uncertainty or evidence that a batter resolves a pitch requires additional validation. In 1,000 sampled 2025 pitches, analytical reconstruction from the stored physics gave median absolute endpoint errors of approximately 0.0029 ft on both axes and 95th-percentile errors below 0.0076 ft. High endpoint accuracy is therefore unsurprising. Define an observation process available at the decision time, compare with the analytical baseline, and validate uncertainty coverage before making perceptual claims.

### 3. P1 Strike probabilities discard the correlation the model learns

`src/lstm_scoring.py:222` multiplies marginal zone probabilities and does not accept rho. Its explanation that an axis-aligned zone makes this valid is false for correlated coordinates. The model explicitly trains rho in its bivariate likelihood, and the visualization uses it.

For a Gaussian centered on the lower-left zone corner with sigma 0.01 ft and rho 0.9, the implementation gives approximately 0.25. The correlated probability is approximately `0.25 + asin(0.9)/(2*pi) = 0.42822`; the distant upper boundaries contribute negligible error. Use a bivariate rectangle integral consistently in scoring and figures.

### 4. P1 Leaderboard baseline predictions receive unequal in-sample treatment

`scripts/train_swing_model.py:65` holds out 20% of batters, trains on the others, and then predicts and scores the entire dataset at lines 118–139. Actual DVA is `(observed_swing - predicted_swing) * (2*P_strike - 1)` (`src/swing_model.py:383`). For training batters, the baseline was fitted to the very decisions whose residuals define their scores; for held-out batters, it was not. This makes the residual comparisons unequal. Generate batter-level out-of-fold baseline predictions for comparable retrospective ratings, with a separate holdout for selecting the model.

### 5. P1 Saved leaderboard results do not match current feature geometry

`scripts/build_swing_features.py:108` now uses rectangular Chebyshev distance. Read-only SQL on Kyle Tucker's 2,439 eligible 2025 pitches reproduces the stored chase rate exactly using the older Euclidean definition: `97/914 = 0.1061269146608315`. Current rectangular geometry gives `59/738 = 0.0799458` instead. Thus the saved leaderboard cannot be treated as output of today's feature code. The stored ratings schema also lacks fields the current training writer produces.

Rebuild dependent features and ratings together after fixing the upstream issues, and record the feature version, model identity, cutoff, and season with each artifact. Matching a presentation table to the database establishes agreement between saved artifacts, not reproducibility from current source.

### 6. P1 A presentation figure labeled as LSTM output is synthetic

Page 19 of `SAGB Conference Presentation - Chris Song.pdf` displays the figure generated by `generate_cloud.py:43`. That script loads no model or pitch data: Gaussian means, covariance matrices, actual-location markers, and probability labels are manually supplied. Its title calls it the LSTM probability cloud at the biological decision point.

The 48% and 94% captions also disagree with the integrals of the supplied distributions over the drawn zone, approximately 50.77% and 99.46%. Label it explicitly as a schematic illustration and correct the probabilities, or replace it with identifiable model predictions.

## Physics and uncertainty implementation

### 7. P2 Movement uncertainty mixes feet and inches

`calibrate.py:93` calculates covariance directly from Statcast `pfx_x` and `pfx_z`. Those fields are in feet according to [Baseball Savant's CSV documentation](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/csv-docs). `src/uncertainty.py:72` treats the corresponding sampled noise as inches and divides by 12 at lines 79–80. The acceleration perturbation from movement is consequently attenuated by 12 in amplitude, or 144 in variance, relative to the stated calibration. This factor concerns the movement component, not the total cloud variance. Keep a single explicit unit convention through calibration and propagation.

### 8. P2 The three uncertainty paths implement incompatible distributions

`src/perception.py:137` documents Bayesian posterior variance `P*R/(P+R)`. Its particle transform at lines 162–163 actually yields variance `(1-K)^2*P`, with `K=P/(P+R)`. With P=R=1, executing the function gives 0.25 instead of the stated posterior variance 0.5. Recenter and rescale deviations to the intended posterior covariance, or sample that posterior correctly.

Separately, `src/scoring.py:33` adds physical and visual variances, making the distribution wider than the physical prior, while the perception module fuses them, making it narrower. The LSTM route uses learned residual variance. These can be alternative models, but must not be documented as interchangeable implementations of the same perceptual cloud.

### 9. P2 Perceptual paths are aligned by row number rather than time or distance

`src/perception.py:112` combines `iloc[i]` from sampled trajectories with the actual pitch's ith point. Perturbed speed changes release-reference time, flight duration, and number of samples. A seeded 100-sample reproduction produced physical paths of 83–90 rows versus 86 actual rows. At index 40, sampled y positions spanned approximately 27.97–29.84 ft. Some perceptual paths ended at y≈2.95 ft instead of the plate at 1.417 ft. `src/visualization.py` then places their final markers on the plate plane regardless.

Interpolate samples onto a common physical time or distance grid, and compute actual plate intersections separately. Do not use the last surviving aligned row as an endpoint.

### 10. P2 Missing release y silently produces invalid cloud trajectories

`src/physics.py` explicitly falls back from missing `release_pos_y` to release extension. `src/uncertainty.py:76` uses `get('release_pos_y', 54.0)`, which does not replace an existing NaN. Its flight-time estimate and perturbed accelerations become NaN. In a reproduction with valid extension and NaN release y, all 10 returned cloud trajectories had nonfinite x/z endpoints, yet were accepted as successful trajectories. Share the fallback logic and reject nonfinite state and endpoint values.

### 11. P2 Simulation failure can be stored as certainty

`scripts/backfill_physical_metrics.py:20` returns `(0.5, 0.0, 0.0)` for an empty cloud. Later scoring ignores that 0.5 fallback probability and uses the zero sigmas. A failed simulation can therefore look physically precise. Store failure status or missing values and exclude failures from fitting rather than encoding them as zero uncertainty.

### 12. P2 Backfills cannot safely resume or rerun

`scripts/backfill_physical_metrics.py:68` declares a pitch primary key, but lines 129 and 134 append every row without checking existing keys or using an upsert. A rerun or restart encounters an integrity error on existing pitches. Use explicit versioned upserts or skip already completed keys; distinguish a deliberate recalculation from a resume. Also record the RNG seed and covariance version for reproducibility.

## Evaluation and score interpretation

### 13. P2 Validation preprocessing uses the validation population

`scripts/train_model.py:205` constructs and normalizes the complete dataset before random splitting at line 213. `src/dataset.py:75` computes statistics across that complete dataset. Fit normalization using training observations only. The random pitch split also does not measure transfer to future games or seasons. The training tensors omit game/date identifiers (`scripts/build_training_data.py:171`), so preserve those identifiers to enable grouped or temporal evaluation.

### 14. P2 Reported endpoint validation does not validate the scoring cutoff

`scripts/train_model.py:110` reports final-timestep R², MAE, and sigma, but `src/lstm_scoring.py:203` evaluates scores at 40% of flight. Streaming NLL does cover valid timesteps during training, but the reported coordinate metrics and sigma summaries do not establish calibration at the deployed decision point. Report endpoint error and interval coverage at the actual cutoff, plus calibration of the derived strike probability.

The two saved LSTM checkpoints, `best_model.pt` and `model_2025_only.pt`, are byte-identical and identify their training years as 2025. There is no saved all-years comparison model; `training_comparison.log` stops before an all-years epoch result. These artifacts do not establish a completed 2025-only versus 2020–2025 comparison.

### 15. P2 Long flights change the meaning of the decision fraction

`scripts/build_training_data.py:96` and `src/lstm_scoring.py:110` retain only the final 100 trajectory samples. `src/model.py:124` then chooses 40% of this retained suffix. For flights longer than roughly 0.495 seconds, that is later than 40% of the full flight. Retain original elapsed time and select a cutoff in physical time, or resample the complete trajectory consistently.

### 16. P2 Confidence normalization does not provide absolute shrinkage

`src/swing_model.py:395` multiplies DVA by confidence, but lines 428–434 divide by total confidence. Uniform confidence cancels from the actual-decision component. A single-context in-memory reproduction with league P(swing)=0.5, all swings, and P(strike)=1 gives actual DVA=0.5 both at n=1 and n=200; n=1 confidence is only 1/31. The reproduction lowers the qualification threshold solely to expose the algebra.

This is relative weighting across a batter's contexts, not absolute shrinkage of the actual component toward the league. The tendency component does undergo posterior shrinkage. Narrow the small-sample claims or implement an aggregation that provides the intended regularization.

### 17. P2 Reported chase rate excludes many outside-zone pitches

`src/swing_model.py:465` uses only `loc_bin == 'chase'`, whereas `scripts/build_swing_features.py:114` puts outside-zone pitches with normalized distance up to 1.5 into `edge`. For Tucker, the current rectangular restricted chase rate is 59/738=7.99%; all outside-zone swings give 324/1,402=23.11%. Rename this restricted statistic or calculate ordinary outside-zone swing rate separately. Keep bin-based posterior summaries distinct from familiar baseball rate labels.

### 18. P2 DVA measures strike agreement rather than optimal baseball value

`src/swing_model.py:375` assigns swing utility P(strike) and take utility 1−P(strike). The implied optimal policy is always swing when P(strike)>0.5. Count, contact quality, whiff probability, and ball/strike consequences do not enter that utility. Count features enter the behavioral baseline, which does not make the utility count-aware. Describe DVA as baseline-adjusted strike-probability agreement unless a baseball utility model is introduced.

The older `FastEyeScorer` similarly fits swing decisions as if P(swing)=P(strike) (`src/scoring.py:112`). Its fitted visual sigma can absorb approach and count effects. It should not be interpreted as an identified visual-acuity measurement.

## Reproducibility and integration

### 19. P2 Current database and notebook schemas are incompatible

Both database paths currently contain only `statcast` and `swing_ratings`; neither has `physical_pitch_metrics` or `eye_ratings`. `scripts/run_eye_rating.py:49` cannot run against that schema, and notebook 05's initial leaderboard query cannot load its required table.

The physical and LSTM rating scripts also share a destructive table writer: `src/db_manager.py:106` replaces `eye_ratings`. The LSTM writer saves `mean_sigma_x` and `mean_sigma_z`, while notebook 05 requires `eye_sigma_ft`. Its saved output already records that KeyError. Other saved cells show incompatible historical Tucker ratings/ranks: 126.4/rank 2 versus 129.1/rank 3. Give each method an explicit schema and migrate or regenerate consumers together.

### 20. P2 Artifacts lose season and model provenance

`scripts/train_swing_model.py:40` accepts arbitrary years, but lines 111–112 always overwrite the same model basename and lines 159–164 replace the entire ratings table without a season field. `export_leaderboard.py:29` always writes a 2025-labeled filename. `get_final_leaderboard.py:18` always claims a 40% cutoff. Feature/model metadata does not reliably preserve that cutoff and source checkpoint identity. Version outputs by season, method, decision time, and upstream artifact hashes; preserve independent runs.

The older physical and LSTM leaderboard scripts additionally standardize all requested batter-season rows together, so adding another year changes an existing season's ratings and ranks. Standardize within season if the intended output is a season-specific leaderboard.

### 21. P2 Clean installation omits core dependencies

`requirements.txt` omits directly used packages including torch, xgboost, plotly, and a Feather/Parquet engine such as pyarrow. Installing the listed dependencies alone does not establish a working training, feature, or visualization pipeline. Declare runtime dependencies and a supported Python environment, with development/test dependencies separately if desired.

## Documentation and visualization inconsistencies

### 22. P2 Presentation formula does not reproduce final ratings

Presentation page 20 defines actual-decision DVA. Current final scores blend 50% tendency DVA and 50% actual DVA and then aggregate with confidence weights (`src/swing_model.py:375`, `:395`, `:428`). Page 21 discusses shrinkage but does not state this blend. Document the exact ranking statistic and distinguish its components. Page 18's correlated-Gaussian explanation must also be reconciled with finding 3.

### 23. P2 Notebook uncertainty figure overlays mirrored paths and two plate planes

Notebook 03, cell 11, calls the helper that negates x, then adds original unmodified trajectories. Saved output contains two Actual Pitch traces starting at −2.04 and +2.04 ft. It also adds a strike plane at y=0 while the helper uses y=1.417. Apply one plotting transform and one plate convention to every layer.

Notebook 02, cell 3, selects the modal pitcher ID but hardcodes the title Blake Snell. The saved ID is 592332, while notebook 03 explicitly identifies Snell as 605483. Derive titles from the selected pitcher's metadata.

### 24. P2 Project guidance describes an obsolete combined pipeline

`CLAUDE.md:12` says FastEyeScorer uses Bayesian perceptual-cloud fusion. It actually uses additive uncertainty around observed plate location; current swing ratings use the separate LSTM/XGBoost/Beta-Binomial route. Document these as three distinct methods, identify the supported entrypoint, and specify which artifacts each produces. Otherwise future edits can accidentally mix incompatible assumptions and schemas.

## Recommended repair order

1. Restore the builder and dependency setup; define explicit schemas and artifact provenance.
2. Decide what observable information and calibration justify the perception interpretation; compare to the analytical endpoint baseline.
3. Fix correlated integration, movement units, posterior covariance, alignment, invalid-state handling, and restart behavior.
4. Implement training-only preprocessing, appropriate evaluation splits, decision-time calibration, and out-of-fold baseline predictions.
5. Resolve score utility, confidence aggregation, and rate definitions; then rebuild features and ratings as one versioned run.
